How to play Hollow Blocks

Controls, scoring, every mode and tips from the foreman · Updated October 7, 2026

The basics

Pieces made of four blocks fall into the well one at a time. Move and turn each one as it falls so that it lands where you want it. When a row across the well is completely filled, it clears and everything above drops down. If the stack reaches the top of the well, the game is over. The next pieces are shown beside the well, a faint "ghost" shows where the current piece will land, and you can put one piece aside in the hold slot (imbak) to swap in later.

Pieces come in a shuffled bag of all seven shapes, so you'll never wait more than twelve pieces for the one you need. Turning uses the standard rotation system with wall kicks, so a piece can turn even when it's pressed against a wall or another piece, and a short lock delay lets you slide a piece after it touches down.

Controls

  • Keyboard: ← and → move; ↓ drops gently; Space drops hard; ↑ or X turns clockwise and Z turns back; C or Shift holds; P pauses; M mutes. Every key can be changed in Settings → Mga pindutan.
  • Phone and tablet: tap to turn, drag sideways to move, drag down for a gentle drop, flick down for a hard drop and flick up to hold. There's also a row of on-screen buttons.
  • Game controller: the d-pad or left stick moves and drops gently, up drops hard, ✕/A turns, ○/B turns back, △/Y or a shoulder button holds, and Options/Start pauses. In the menus the d-pad moves between buttons.

Scoring

Your score is your kita, counted in pesos. Clearing one, two, three or four rows at once pays 100, 300, 500 or 800, multiplied by your level. Four rows at once is a Bayanihan. A T-spin (turning the T-shaped piece into a slot that it couldn't have dropped into straight) pays extra. Doing a Bayanihan or a T-spin twice in a row is back-to-back and pays half again. Clearing rows with several pieces in a row builds a combo bonus. Clearing the whole well at once, a perfect clear, pays the most of all.

Tips from Kapatas

  • Keep it flat. A flat stack gives every piece somewhere to go. Avoid tall towers and narrow pits you can only fill with the long piece.
  • Leave one column open for Bayanihans. Stack nine columns cleanly and keep the tenth empty; when the long piece comes, drop it in for four rows at once.
  • Use the hold. If the piece you have doesn't fit, hold it and take the next one. Keep the long piece in hold for when your open column is ready.
  • Look ahead. The next pieces are shown for a reason. Plan where the next two or three will go, not only the one that's falling.
  • Learn the T-spin. Build a slot shaped like a T with an overhang, then turn the T piece into it at the last moment. The Pagsasanay lessons walk you through singles, doubles and triples with a guide showing where the piece goes.
  • Don't panic at speed. Higher floors fall faster. Hard drop as soon as you've lined a piece up, so you have more time for the next one.

The modes, and how to win them

  • Bahay (House): endless, and every ten rows is a new floor. Play for Bayanihans: they pay the most and raise the house fastest. Every fifth floor finishes a house that goes into your collection book, where you can open it and share it as a picture.
  • Klasiko (Classic): the same rules at one steady speed for the whole game, chosen by difficulty. It's the cleanest test of your stacking, with its own ranking.
  • Deadline: clear forty rows against the clock. Speed matters more than score here, so take singles and doubles when they keep the stack low, and hard drop everything.
  • Bagyo (Storm): mud rises from below with a single gap in each row, faster as the storm goes on. Clear rows through the gaps to dig down, and keep your stack low so the mud has room to come.
  • Karera (Race): two minutes for the most kita. Bayanihans and back-to-back bonuses add up fast, so build for them early while there's time.
  • Daily: two minutes on the same pieces as everyone else that day (by Manila date). Your best is kept, and you can share a spoiler-free result with friends.
  • Lingguhan (Weekly): each week has a twist: Storm Week, Earthquake Week, Tools Only, Fast (pieces fall at once) or Retro (no hold, no ghost, one next piece, no wall kicks). Finish one to earn its medal.
  • Proyekto (Projects): thirty contracts. Each has a goal (clear so many rows, reach a floor in time, make T-spins, outlast the mud) and a twist (only two holds, earthquakes, power cuts that darken the well, wind that pushes pieces sideways, cracked blocks that crumble). Beat a contract for a star; beat its par for two or three. Stars open new barangays, and the last job is the City Inspector, who stamps REJECTED blocks on your stack while you race to finish three floors.
  • Laban (Versus): you and a rival build side by side. Clearing two or more rows sends mud rows to the rival's well, and theirs come to you; your own clears cancel incoming mud first. Climb the Barangay League by beating six rivals in turn, each with their own pace and style.
  • Tapatan (Head to Head): two people on one computer, best of three rounds, with handicaps to even out a match between a beginner and an expert.
  • Pagsasanay (Training): Kapatas's lessons on T-spins, the four-wide combo and finesse (placing each piece in as few key presses as possible).

Coins, the shop and your progress

Every game pays barya (coins) for the rows, Bayanihans and T-spins you make. Spend them in the Tindahan (shop) on block skins (glazed tiles, capiz shell, neon, Christmas lanterns, gold), outfits for Kapatas and new building sites: the Makati business district, a beach resort or the rice fields of the province. Come back each day to keep your streak going for a small bonus. Your best runs are saved as replays, and in Deadline and Race you can race a ghost of your best.

Common questions

  • Is it free? Yes. Everything in the game can be earned by playing.
  • Do I need an account? No. An optional account (a name and a password, no email) keeps your progress if you clear your browser, and puts you on the ranking boards.
  • Can I play offline? Yes. After your first visit the game is saved on your device and runs without a connection; rankings sync when you're back online.
  • Can I play in English? Yes, under Settings → Wika · Language.
